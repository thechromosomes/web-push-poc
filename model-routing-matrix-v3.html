Routing economics — rules of thumb

80/20 default: route ~80% of work to T1–T2 (Composer / Sonnet); reserve T3–T4 for the ~20% that genuinely needs it. Defaulting everything to Opus/GPT-5.5 typically pays a large premium for marginal gain.

Cache aggressively: stable system prompts and large repo context cached on GPT-5.5 (input drops ~90%) and Opus materially cut the effective bill for long-running agents.

Escalate, don't start high: two failed Sonnet attempts is the trigger to move to T3–T4 — not the starting point.

Azure-only constraint

Confirm GPT-5.5 and each Claude tier are live in your Azure / Foundry region before locking this in — vendor GA dates lead Azure availability by some weeks, and list prices can differ from your negotiated Azure rate.

Recommendation

Default swap: GPT-5.2-Codex wherever the original matrix says Composer 2.5 — it is the true functional equivalent (agentic, IDE/CLI-agnostic, Azure-available) at a modest premium.

If you'd rather stay all-Anthropic: use Haiku 4.5 for the rote T1 work and Sonnet 4.6 as the "little pricier" daily driver. This keeps one vendor, one billing surface, and avoids the GPT-5.4 sunset risk.

Avoid for now: Gemini 3 Flash / Flash-Lite are cheaper still and strong on coding, but Google models are not on Azure — they'd break your single-cloud routing constraint.

If You're on GitHub Copilot

Copilot doesn't bill per token in the chat/agent experience — it bills premium requests, each model carrying a multiplier against your monthly allowance. The routing logic still holds, but "cost tier" now means multiplier, not $/M. Three structural differences change the picture.

What changes vs. raw API

1. Composer 2.5 is irrelevant here — it's Cursor-only and never appears in Copilot. Use GPT-5.2-Codex or Haiku 4.5 in its place.

2. Gemini is gone — all Gemini models were removed from Copilot Chat on the web (May 2026). Verify your IDE picker, but don't plan around Gemini.

3. Auto mode picks the model from a 1x-or-lower pool and gives a 10% multiplier discount in Chat — a good default for routine work; override manually for the hard 20%.

Practical guidance

For most everyday work, use Auto or Sonnet 4.6. They do the job well and only cost you 1 request each, so you won't run through your monthly allowance quickly.

For simple, repetitive jobs, switch to Haiku 4.5. It only costs a third of a request, so you can do about three times as much before using up your allowance.

Save GPT-5.5 and Opus 4.7 for the hard problems. They're powerful but expensive: a single Opus request uses up as much of your allowance as roughly 45 Haiku requests. Only reach for them when Sonnet has actually tried and failed.

Can't find a model in the list? On Business and Enterprise plans, your IT admin has to switch each model on before it shows up. If one you expect is missing, ask them to enable it.

Copilot's available models and multipliers change frequently and differ by surface (web chat vs VS Code vs JetBrains vs CLI) and plan. Verify in your own model picker.

Pricing and model availability are fast-moving — re-validate quarterly. List rates from vendor pricing pages, June 2026; your enterprise/Azure rates may differ.
